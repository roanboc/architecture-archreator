How it works

You decide. It writes. Nothing skips a step.

archreator is a set of instructions your AI agent follows. It changes what the agent does before it builds — and what it has to come back and ask you.

The one rule everything else serves

A change in what you want is never coded directly. It is aligned through the layers, approved by you at the gates, written down — and only then built.

Pure bug fixes skip all of it. If nothing you documented changes, there is nothing to align, and the method gets out of the way.

The layers

Six answers, in an order that holds

Each layer answers one question, and answers it before the layer below can be right. That order is the reason a change can be traced from "why" all the way to a file.

  1. 0Business design — who we serve and what we offer Filled only when the subject is an organization: the customer segments, what they are trying to get done, what hurts, and the products that relieve it. Everything below is derived from here.
  2. 1Strategy — what must become true Stakeholders and the pressures on them, goals, the principles a future change gets checked against, and the capabilities the organization needs to have.
  3. 2Business — who acts, and what is offered Actors and roles — each one stated as human, AI, or both — plus the services, the channels, and the rules that constrain them.
  4. 3Information — what is known The data that exists, where it lives, and how sensitive it is. Often the layer that reveals you are storing something you never decided to store.
  5. 4Application — the software Services and components, each naming the module or file that implements it. This is where the model becomes checkable against the code.
  6. 5Technology — what it runs on Runtimes, hosting, the build and the pipeline. For most small projects, refreshingly short.

A small app does not need all six filled. You declare a depth at the start — one application, one organization, or an enterprise split into business lines — and the method sizes itself to it.

The gates

Four moments where the agent has to stop

A gate is not a form. It is the agent presenting what it understood, in your language, with links to the exact documents — and waiting.

GATE 0

The business model

Who you serve and how the offer works, before anything is derived from it. Only when you are modeling an organization rather than a single app.

GATE 1

The strategy

The goals, the principles, and what the organization must be able to do — before the business layer is built on top of them.

GATE 2

The business change

What will actually change, layer by layer, with explicit "no change" verdicts where nothing moves. No code is written before this one.

GATE 3

The solution design

Optional, and only if you ask for it at Gate 2 — the components and patterns before they are implemented.

Whatever you say is transcribed into the change's own record: which gate, who approved, on what date, against which documents. An approval that is not written down did not happen.

The AI's place

The agent is modeled as a member of the organization

Not a tool someone uses — an actor with a name, a role, and limits written into the model itself.

Every role in an archreator model states whether a human holds it, an AI holds it, or both. Where an AI is involved, three more things are recorded, in concrete terms:

  • Autonomy level — advisory, co-pilot, autonomous with a checkpoint, or fully autonomous.
  • Decision rights — what it is actually allowed to decide or change.
  • Escalation path — the named role it hands off to when it is out of its depth.

Changing any of those is itself a decision that gets recorded, with the reasoning. That is what stops autonomy from creeping upward one convenient exception at a time.

What you end up with

Files, in your repository, that outlive the session

The model

One folder per layer, markdown tables and diagrams. Every element has an identifier other documents refer to, so nothing is described twice and nothing silently disagrees.

One record per change

What changed, in which layers, what was deliberately left out, and the approvals. Merged records are never rewritten — the history stays true about what was known at the time.

Checks that run

Two small scripts verify that every link and every cross-reference resolves. A model that references something deleted three changes ago fails loudly instead of misleading the next agent quietly.
